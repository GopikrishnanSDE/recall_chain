## End-to-end checks (outside Hardhat tests)

Two further checks were run against a local Hardhat node, the Express server and the production Next.js build:

- **API + client library (20 checks):** batch codes generated by the client library match the contract's leaf formula; the server rejects leaf uploads that do not rebuild the on-chain root; no secrets reach the server; verify / claim / recall / closure behave as specified through the HTTP API.
- **Browser (31 checks):** roles registered and stages advanced through the existing pages; batch creation with CSV download; customer verify and claim without a wallet; "already used" warning on a second device; recall, quarantine, confirmation and escalation through the Recalls page; recall alert on the customer's device; closure report numbers match the contract; no page errors.

## Interpretation

These results show that the specified authorization rules, state transitions and accounting behave as asserted on a local test network. They do not measure latency or throughput, and they cannot show that the physical world matches what participants report on-chain (for example, that quarantined packs were really pulled from shelves). Gas figures are execution costs on a local chain; on a public chain, per-pack operations (`quarantineUnits`, claims) would be the dominant cost. Any paper claim should describe this as a local functional test and gas snapshot, not production performance data.
